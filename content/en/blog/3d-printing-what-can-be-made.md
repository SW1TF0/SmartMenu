# 3D printing: what can be made and when it pays off

A knob on an appliance breaks and the spare part is no longer sold. You need a box made to an exact size. You have an idea and want to hold it in your hand before ordering production. In cases like these, 3D printing is often the quickest route. At SmartMenuKJ we print parts, prototypes and small batches in Kardzhali. Here's what can be made, when it pays off and how to prepare.

## How it works, briefly
A 3D printer builds an object layer by layer from plastic, following a digital model. The model can come from a drawing, from measurements or from a **sample**: an existing part we measure or **3D scan**. So you don't need a ready file; bring the part or describe the idea.

## What can be made

### Spare parts that are no longer sold
This is the most common reason people come to us. Knobs, clips, brackets, caps and holders on appliances, furniture and cars are often the first to break, long after the manufacturer stopped selling them. We measure the part, model it and print it in a durable material. One of our projects: we reproduced a discontinued bracket and printed a whole batch.

### Prototypes
If you're developing a product, a prototype lets you check size, shape and handling before paying for large-scale production. Make a version, try it, improve it.

### Boxes and holders made to measure
**Custom enclosures** for electronics, **holders, hooks and organisers** for home and office. When nothing in the shop fits exactly, a made-to-measure part solves it.

### Small batches
Need ten or fifty identical parts rather than thousands? **Small production runs** are a strength of 3D printing, because there's no expensive mould as in injection moulding.

### Gifts, figurines and hobby parts
**Souvenirs, figurines**, parts for **models and hobbies**, decorative items, where colour and finish matter as much as strength.

## Which material for what
We choose the material based on what the part is for:
- **PLA**: easy to print, good for models, prototypes and decorative items that don't take load;
- **PETG**: tougher and more resistant, good for functional parts and holders;
- **ABS**: for parts that must cope with higher temperatures and knocks;
- **TPU**: flexible, rubber-like, for seals, tips and protectors.

Not sure which? Tell us where the part will sit and what it has to withstand, and we'll suggest the right one.

## When 3D printing pays off
- **The part is no longer sold**, or the wait for it is weeks.
- **You need one or a few pieces**, not mass production.
- **The shape is non-standard** and there's nothing similar in the shops.
- **You want to test an idea** before investing more.

When is it not the best choice? When you need thousands of identical pieces or the part must be metal. Then we'll tell you honestly.

## How to prepare
1. **Bring the sample**, even if it's broken: the pieces help with measuring.
2. **Take a photo of where the part sits** if you can't remove it.
3. **Tell us what it does**: does it carry weight, get hot, need to flex?
4. **Tell us how many** you need.

Then you get a clear written quote within 24 hours, with no obligation. We send you a prototype or photos along the way, and the consultation is free.
